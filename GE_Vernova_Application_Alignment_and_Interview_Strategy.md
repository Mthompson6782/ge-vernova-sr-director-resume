## 4. Executive Interview Prep & Strategic Talking Points

When interviewing with GE Vernova’s CISO, CTO, and DT Supply Chain Executives, anticipate questions focused on the tension between security and manufacturing uptime. Use these structured responses:

### Q1: "How do you balance Safety, Quality, Delivery, and Cost (SQDC) when deploying security controls in live manufacturing plants?"
* **Strategic Answer Framework:**  
  "In Enterprise IT, the triad is Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability. On the plant floor, that priority is completely inverted to **Availability, Integrity, and Safety (AIC)**. As a Six Sigma Master Black Belt and former plant automation leader, I view security through an operational lens:
  1. **Safety & Availability Non-Negotiables:** No security agent, scan, or firewall rule should ever trip a turbine test, interrupt a continuous process, or jeopardize worker safety. Active network scanning on Level 1/Level 2 PLC networks is strictly prohibited in favor of passive OT monitoring (Claroty/Nozomi/Dragos) and SPAN/TAP analysis.
  2. **Delivery & Cycle Time:** Security controls must not introduce operational friction. When we design remote access for OEM field engineers or plant technicians, we build frictionless Jumpbox bastions with automated session recording and MFA that integrate with ServiceNow CMDB workflows.
  3. **Cost & Standard Work:** Rather than demanding rip-and-replace of multimillion-dollar legacy machinery, we establish EVM standard work that leverages network isolation, Level 3.5 DMZs, and unidirectional data diodes as compensating controls."

### Q2: "How do you handle legacy, unpatchable OT assets (e.g., Windows XP/7 HMIs or proprietary PLCs) that cannot be taken offline?"
* **Strategic Answer Framework:**  
  "This is one of the core challenges addressed in NIST SP 800-82 Rev 3. In global manufacturing, asking an operations leader to patch an embedded controller that hasn't had an outage in 4 years—or for which the OEM has no certified patch—is a non-starter.
  * We implement **defense-in-depth compensating controls**:
    1. **Strict Micro-Segmentation:** Isolate the legacy controller behind an industrial firewall or dedicated VLAN conduit so it only communicates with designated Level 2 HMIs via explicit IP/port/protocol pairings.
    2. **Read-Only Gateways:** Where data must leave the cell for analytics or ServiceNow tracking, enforce unidirectional or read-only Modbus/OPC communication.
    3. **Host Hardening:** Disable unnecessary services, USB ports, and network protocols on the supervisory HMI, deploying tested EDR (CrowdStrike in audit/quarantine mode) only where validated by the OEM.
    4. **Maintenance Alignment:** Schedule firmware updates exclusively during planned major plant turnarounds, strictly using OEM-validated release packages."

### Q3: "GE Vernova has extensive research and engineering test labs connected to the enterprise. How do you formalize lab security without stifling R&D agility?"
* **Strategic Answer Framework:**  
  "At MITRE and in high-tech test environments, engineering labs require maximum agility: engineers are constantly testing novel protocols, hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) simulators, and pre-production firmware.
  * The solution is **Zone-Conduit Containment**:
    1. Treat connected labs as semi-trusted, partitioned zones within the Purdue model. They must never share flat networking with the enterprise or production plant networks.
    2. Build dedicated Lab DMZs with granular ingress/egress filtering so R&D engineers can safely pull test data or telemetry without creating backdoors into core DT systems.
    3. Implement ephemeral, automated sandbox environments where external vendor hardware can be tested in isolation before connecting to enterprise-wide infrastructure."

### Q4: "How do you report OT cyber risk to the Executive Leadership Team (ELT) without sounding like Chicken Little?"
* **Strategic Answer Framework:**  
  "Executives do not want raw CVE counts or technical vulnerability scores. They want to know: *What is our operational exposure, what is our time-to-recover, and how does this affect revenue and delivery?*
  * I translate OT risk into business KPIs:
    1. **Mean Time to Contain (MTTC)** in the event of an operational anomaly.
    2. **Segmentation Maturity Index:** Percentage of plants and labs fully segmented behind validated Level 3.5 DMZs.
    3. **Asset Visibility Coverage:** Percentage of active Level 0–3 assets inventoried in ServiceNow CMDB with verified owners.
    4. **Critical Asset Protection Ratio:** Percentage of high-impact crown-jewel processes with active compensating controls and verified offline backups."
